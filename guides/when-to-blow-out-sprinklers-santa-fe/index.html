When to Blow Out Sprinklers in Santa Fe

Santa Fe's first fall freeze usually arrives in the first half of October. Water left in sprinkler lines, valves and the backflow device can freeze, expand and crack them. Here's how to time your blowout and what a proper one includes.

By Bradley Babb, irrigation specialist & permaculturist · Updated November 2026

Key takeaways

  • Plan your blowout for late September to mid-October, before the first hard freeze.
  • Santa Fe's average first fall frost is around October 10–11; higher spots like Eldorado and Arroyo Hondo can freeze earlier.
  • Draining alone often leaves water in low spots and valves. Compressed air clears it.
  • The backflow device is the part most often damaged by freezing, and the most expensive to replace.

When is the first freeze in Santa Fe?

Long-term climate data puts Santa Fe's average first fall frost around October 10–11, with the last spring frost usually in May. Those are averages, so any given year can run one to three weeks earlier or later. Elevation matters too: homes on higher ground or open lots southeast of town often see frost sooner than the valley floor in Pojoaque or Tesuque.

A light frost won't usually hurt buried pipe. The risk comes from hard freezes, nights in the mid-20s or colder, which freeze water in above-ground parts: the backflow preventer, valve manifolds near the surface, and drip lines on the ground.

Why draining isn't enough

Many systems have manual drain valves, and opening them helps. But water settles in low spots, valve bodies and sprinkler heads that sit lower than the drains. That leftover water is what freezes and splits fittings. A blowout pushes regulated compressed air through each zone until the heads run dry, clearing water that gravity can't.

What a proper blowout includes

  • Shutting off the irrigation supply and relieving pressure
  • Clearing each zone, one at a time, with air at a safe pressure for the pipe and heads
  • Clearing drip zones at low pressure so tubing and emitters aren't blown apart
  • Draining and protecting the backflow device
  • Setting the controller to off or rain mode for winter
  • Noting anything that needs repair before spring start-up

Can I do it myself?

You can, if you have a compressor that moves enough air and you keep the pressure in check. Too much pressure can damage heads, fittings and drip lines, and running a zone dry for too long heats up the pipe. Never stand over the backflow device while blowing out, and never blow air back through it. If you're unsure, a pro visit usually costs less than replacing a cracked backflow device.

Spring start-up

In spring, wait until hard freezes are past, then turn the water on slowly so a surge doesn't slam the pipes. Run each zone and look for geysers, puddles and dry spots: those are the leaks winter left behind.

Questions

Quick answers

When should I blow out my sprinklers in Santa Fe?

Before the first hard freeze, usually late September to mid-October. Santa Fe's average first fall frost is around October 10–11, and higher areas can freeze earlier.

Do drip systems need to be winterized?

Yes. Drip lines, filters and valves can crack when water freezes in them. Clear them at low pressure and drain any above-ground parts.

What happens if I don't winterize?

Water left in the system can freeze and crack pipes, valves, sprinkler heads and the backflow device, which often shows up as leaks or a geyser at spring start-up.
