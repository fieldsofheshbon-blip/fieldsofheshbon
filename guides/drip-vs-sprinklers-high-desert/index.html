Drip vs. Sprinklers in the High Desert

In Northern New Mexico, most of a landscape is better off on drip. Spray still has a place, mainly small lawns. Here's how to decide, and why converting usually saves water and keeps you within Santa Fe's watering rules.

By Bradley Babb, irrigation specialist & permaculturist · Updated November 2026

Key takeaways

  • Drip puts water at the roots, so far less is lost to wind, sun and overspray.
  • City of Santa Fe rules don't allow overhead spray on trees and shrubs, and prohibit overspray and runoff.
  • Spray makes sense mainly for turf. Trees, shrubs, beds and gardens belong on drip.
  • Caliche and clay soak slowly, so drip's slow delivery suits them.

How each one works

Spray irrigation throws water through the air in arcs or rotating streams. It covers turf evenly, but in dry, windy air a real share evaporates or drifts before it lands, and on hard soil it can run off before it soaks in.

Drip irrigation delivers water slowly through emitters or drip line right at the base of each plant. Less water is lost, foliage stays dry, and the slow rate lets water sink into tight soils instead of sheeting off.

What Santa Fe's rules say

The City of Santa Fe's seasonal rules (May 1 through October 31) ban outdoor watering between 10 a.m. and 6 p.m., prohibit overspray and runoff, and don't allow overhead spray irrigation for trees and shrubs. In practice, any trees and shrubs still on spray heads should be moved to drip, and the controller should run early morning or evening.

When spray still makes sense

Spray is the right tool for a lawn, where you need even coverage over a solid area. Even then, high-efficiency rotary nozzles, correct spacing and cycle-and-soak programming (shorter repeat runs) cut waste a lot.

Converting spray zones to drip

Many spray zones can be converted at the valve: add a filter and pressure regulator, cap or replace the heads, and run drip line or emitters to each plant. It's one of the most cost-effective upgrades for a high-desert yard, and it keeps overspray off walls, windows and walkways.

Keeping drip working

Drip needs a little care: flush lines once or twice a year, clean the filter, check that emitters still drip, and add emitters as plants grow. Most "drip doesn't work" complaints trace back to a clogged filter or emitters that were never moved out as trees grew.

Questions

Quick answers

Is drip or sprinkler irrigation better in Santa Fe?

For trees, shrubs, beds and gardens, drip is better: it loses less water and fits city rules. Spray is mainly for lawns.

Can I water trees with sprinklers in Santa Fe?

City of Santa Fe rules don't allow overhead spray irrigation for trees and shrubs. Converting those areas to drip is the fix.

How long should I run drip?

Longer and less often than spray so water soaks deep. The right time depends on emitter flow, soil and season.
